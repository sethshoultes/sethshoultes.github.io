Aerodynamics in Drone Design

2. Propeller Design and Aerodynamics

{% raw %}

Propeller Shape and Lift

Propellers are the primary source of lift for drones, and their design significantly impacts aerodynamics.

  • Blade Angle (Pitch): The pitch of a propeller blade refers to the angle at which it cuts through the air. A steeper pitch moves more air per revolution, generating more thrust but requiring more power. A lower pitch provides less lift but improves efficiency, especially at lower speeds.
  • Blade Length: Longer blades can move more air, generating greater lift, but they increase drag, making the drone slower to accelerate and respond.
  • Blade Shape: Modern drones use airfoil-shaped blades, which are designed to create high lift while minimizing drag. The airfoil shape works by forcing air to move faster over the top surface than the bottom, generating upward lift.

Example:

If you want your drone to fly faster and perform aggressive maneuvers (like in drone racing), you’ll want to use propellers with a steeper pitch and shorter blades for quick thrust and better responsiveness. On the other hand, longer, lower-pitch propellers are ideal for stable hovering and long-distance flight, where efficiency is prioritized.

Number of Blades

  • Two-bladed propellers are more efficient and generate less drag, making them ideal for drones designed for longer flight times.
  • Three or four-bladed propellers generate more thrust but create more drag, leading to quicker battery consumption. They are used for racing or situations requiring fast, powerful acceleration.
