Academy / Drone Piloting: Getting Started

Drone Piloting: Advanced Concepts

First-Person View (FPV) Flying

{% raw %}

FPV drones give you a real-time feed from the drone’s perspective, often used in racing or exploration of hard-to-reach places.

  • FPV Equipment: Requires FPV goggles, a transmitter, and a camera mounted on the drone.
  • Manual Control: FPV flying often requires more advanced manual control, as you may need to navigate through tight spaces at high speeds.
    • Acro Mode: Unlike GPS or altitude hold modes, Acro Mode (short for acrobatic) doesn’t auto-level the drone, giving you full control for performing flips, rolls, and aggressive maneuvers.

Example:

FPV racing is an exciting way to test your skills. You’ll fly through obstacle courses at high speeds, requiring precision and fast reflexes. The drone can reach speeds of 70-90 mph (112-145 km/h), making it both challenging and thrilling!
