Search and Rescue: Stable Hovering and Maneuvering in Windy Conditions

{% raw %}

Pro Pilots: Drones used for search and rescue operations must be stable, even in harsh conditions, such as high winds or around obstacles like trees and cliffs. Stability is key when hovering to search for missing persons or dropping supplies.

Key Techniques:

  • Altitude Hold and GPS Lock: In windy environments, pro rescue pilots often use altitude hold and GPS lock to maintain a stable position while scanning an area. However, in tight spaces or areas with weak GPS signals (like canyons), they rely on manual hovering techniques.
  • Manual Hovering: Pilots skilled in manual hovering use minimal stick movements to compensate for the wind, ensuring that the drone stays in place while the camera provides a clear, steady view of the terrain.

Example Video:

In search and rescue simulations, you'll see drones hovering steadily while inspecting a disaster site, despite strong wind gusts. The pilot continuously adjusts yaw and pitch to maintain a stable hover while avoiding obstacles like trees or debris.
