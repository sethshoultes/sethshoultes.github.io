Certification Study Guides: FAA Part 107 and EU A2 Certificate

3. General Study Tips for Both FAA Part 107 and EU A2 Certificate

{% raw %}

Understand Airspace Classifications:

For both the FAA Part 107 and EU A2 exams, you’ll need a solid grasp of airspace classifications and flight restrictions. Here’s a breakdown of how they compare:

  • FAA Part 107: Focuses heavily on Class B, C, D, and E airspace. You need to know how to request airspace authorization using LAANC (Low Altitude Authorization and Notification Capability).
  • EU A2: Concentrates on restricted airspaces in urban areas and flight height restrictions (max altitude of 120 meters). Make sure you can interpret airspace restrictions on VFR maps.

METAR and TAF Reports:

Understanding weather reports is a must for both certifications. Familiarize yourself with the abbreviations used in METAR and TAF reports. Here’s a quick reference:

  • METAR: Provides real-time weather observations, such as wind direction, visibility, cloud cover, and temperature.
  • TAF: Provides weather forecasts, typically issued for a 24-hour or 30-hour period.

Weight and Balance:

Both exams emphasize the importance of understanding how payload and center of gravity affect flight performance. Ensure you can calculate how the addition of a camera or other equipment changes the drone’s balance and battery life.

Operational Limitations:

  • Part 107: The FAA emphasizes the need to maintain line of sight, follow daylight operations, and stay below 400 feet.
  • A2 Certificate: EU regulations are stricter when flying near people and in urban areas. You’ll need to know when to use low-speed mode or apply for specific operational authorizations.
