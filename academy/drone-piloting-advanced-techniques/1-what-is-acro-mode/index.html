Advanced Techniques: Mastering Acro Mode for Full Manual Control

What is Acro Mode?

{% raw %}

Acro Mode, short for Acrobatic Mode, is the ultimate challenge for drone pilots. It removes all the automated stabilization features that most drones provide, allowing you to take full manual control over the drone’s flight dynamics. This mode is used heavily in FPV (First-Person View) drone racing, freestyle flying, and aerial acrobatics.

When you’re ready to move into Acro Mode, you’ll need to master how to control yaw, pitch, and roll independently without any assistance from the drone’s autopilot or GPS stabilization systems. Let's break down the techniques and strategies to help you succeed in this advanced flight mode.

In Acro Mode, your drone no longer self-levels or stabilizes itself. Unlike Angle Mode or Horizon Mode, which keep the drone level when you release the control sticks, Acro Mode lets the drone maintain its last orientation, regardless of whether it’s level, inverted, or spinning.

Key Characteristics of Acro Mode:

  • No auto-leveling: You are in full control of keeping the drone stable in the air. Once you tilt the drone, it will continue to tilt until you correct it.
  • Fast Response: Acro Mode provides immediate and precise feedback to your inputs, making the drone extremely responsive but also more difficult to control.
  • Full 360-degree movement: You can perform flips, rolls, and spins with no restrictions.

Example:

When flying in Acro Mode, if you tilt the drone forward (pitch), it will keep tilting until you pull back on the stick to stop it. If you don’t make any adjustments, the drone could flip upside down, or worse, crash. This is why constant control input is necessary.
