FAA Part 107 Certification Study Guide (United States)

{% raw %}

The FAA Part 107 certification allows you to legally operate drones for commercial purposes in the U.S. Here’s a breakdown of the topics covered in the exam, along with resources to help you prepare.

Part 107 Exam Topics

  1. Regulations:
    • Airspace classifications, restrictions, and operational limits (e.g., where you can and can’t fly).
    • Rules for flying near airports, controlled airspace (Class B, C, D, and E), and NOTAMs (Notice to Airmen).
    • Registration requirements and remote pilot responsibilities.
  2. Airspace and Weather:
    • How to read aeronautical charts (Sectional Charts) and identify no-fly zones.
    • Understanding different types of airspace classifications (Class B, C, D, E, and G).
    • Weather patterns, cloud formations, and how weather affects drone performance.
  3. Flight Operations:
    • Safe flying practices, emergency procedures, and pre-flight checks.
    • Remote Pilot-in-Command (RPIC) responsibilities, including collision avoidance.
    • Visual Line of Sight (VLOS) and rules for Beyond Visual Line of Sight (BVLOS) operations.
  4. Aeronautical Decision-Making:
    • Understanding risk management, decision-making models, and crew resource management.
  5. Performance and Loading:
    • Understanding weight and balance, center of gravity, and drone performance during flight.
  6. Radio Communication:
    • Basics of radio communication with air traffic control and understanding common ATC phrases.

Study Tips for Part 107:

  • Use FAA’s Study Resources: The FAA provides an official Remote Pilot – Small UAS Study Guide, which covers all exam topics in detail. Download it here.
  • Practice with Aeronautical Charts: Learn how to read sectional charts, especially identifying restricted airspace and no-fly zones. A good interactive tool is SkyVector, which provides real-time sectional charts.
  • Understand Weather Effects: The FAA will test your knowledge of how weather affects drones, particularly how to read METAR (Meteorological Aerodrome Report) and TAF (Terminal Aerodrome Forecast). Brush up on aviation weather reports.
  • Practice Tests: Take as many Part 107 practice tests as possible. Many online resources provide full-length exams to help you get comfortable with the format.
