Immelmann Turn: The Fastest Way to Change Direction in Style

1. What is an Immelmann Turn?

{% raw %}

The Immelmann Turn is a highly advanced maneuver that allows drone pilots to rapidly change direction while gaining altitude. This move is borrowed from traditional aviation and is commonly used in aerial combat and FPV (First-Person View) drone racing. Named after World War I German fighter pilot Max Immelmann, this maneuver combines a half-loop with a half-roll to efficiently reverse direction without losing momentum.

The Immelmann Turn is perfect for fast transitions in tight spaces, quick getaways, and impressive aerial acrobatics. In FPV drone racing, this move is particularly valuable when navigating courses with sharp turns or vertical elements that require a swift change in direction.

An Immelmann Turn consists of two key actions:

  1. Half-Loop: The drone pitches backward to climb upward in a loop.
  2. Half-Roll: When the drone reaches the top of the loop, it performs a half-roll to return to level flight while facing in the opposite direction.

Key Benefits of an Immelmann Turn:

  • Rapid Direction Change: Perfect for reversing your flight path while maintaining forward momentum.
  • Gaining Altitude: You gain altitude during the half-loop, making it useful for avoiding obstacles or gaining a tactical advantage in drone racing.
  • Fluid Movement: The Immelmann Turn is designed for smooth, flowing flight, which is essential for maintaining speed in tight race courses or cinematic shots.

Example:

If you’re racing through a track and need to turn 180 degrees while gaining altitude, the Immelmann Turn allows you to make the transition efficiently without stopping or losing speed.
