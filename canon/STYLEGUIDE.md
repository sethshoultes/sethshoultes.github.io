# sethshoultes.com: style guide

## The words

- Seth's own plain voice (`scripts/voices/seth.md` is the full guide the blog writer uses): talk to one person, "you" and "we";
  open with what happened; a headline is a sentence you would say out loud; one idea, carried all the way through.
- A specific detail beats a general claim: a real date, a real number, a real file. Insider words (worktree, webhook, schema)
  get one plain sentence before they are leaned on.
- Pages that sell say who it is for and what it costs in plain words. Prices and plan names are Seth's to change.
- No throat-clearing, no corporate or academic framing, no borrowed literary voice.

## The look

- **Colours** (`assets/brand.css`): ground `#0b0d10`, surfaces `#14171c` and `#1b1f26`, text `#f4f4f5`, muted `#a1a7b0`,
  border `#262b33`, and one accent, orange `#ff6b2c`. The accent marks the one thing to do on a page; it is not decoration.
- **Type:** Inter, with a metric-matched Arial fallback so the page does not jump while the font loads.
- **Layout:** one content column; cards line up with it (#152). Motion is quiet and never needed to read the page.

## The pictures

Real screenshots, real places in Southern Utah and diagrams of real systems. Each image has alt text that says what is in it.
No stock photos of people and no generated people. A blog post's featured image is made for that post.
