--- title: "Your Price Is Not Your Cost Plus a Hope" subtitle: "A simple pricing idea from Profit First for Contractors, in my own words." description: "A simple pricing idea from Profit First for Contractors, in my own words." read_time: "4 min read" ---

I have been reading material from Shawn Van Dyke's Profit First for Contractors, a course for the construction trades. One idea in it applies to almost any business that sells work: price is built from your costs, on purpose.

The course puts it as one equation: price equals costs times a markup factor. The costs are what you pay to deliver the work, like labor, materials, subcontractors and equipment. The markup factor is the number you multiply by. If you want to know yours, divide what you take in by what you spent delivering the work.

Two things from that are worth remembering.

Margin and markup are not the same. Markup is measured against your cost. Margin is measured against your price. A business that confuses the two can think it is earning more than it is. As the course says, you do not sell your cost, you sell your price.

Nobody can hand you your numbers. The course is blunt that industry standards do not exist: your business is unique to your market, your services and the value you deliver, and any rule of thumb is just a broad guide to test against your own books.

So if you are quoting by feel, try this once. Add up what it truly costs to deliver one job, decide what your business needs to keep, and work backward to the price. If the price feels too high for your market, you have learned something real: either costs are too high, or the service needs to be worth more to the customer.

This is my own summary of an idea from Shawn Van Dyke's course. The formulas and tables are his, so go to the source for those.
