--- title: "Saying No to Custom Requests Without Losing the Customer" subtitle: "How to keep your product focused when someone wants something only they need." description: "How to keep your product focused when someone wants something only they need." read_time: "4 min read" ---

Sooner or later a customer asks for something just for them. It is a fair request, and it might even be a good idea. But if you build everything everyone asks for, your product slowly stops being good for anyone.

Here is what worked for us at Event Espresso.

Show where you are going. We used a public roadmap, with a tool like Trello, so customers could see what we were building and why. Many "can you add this?" questions answer themselves when the plan is in plain sight.

Keep your own vision in front of the team. I kept a high-level roadmap from the start and shared it with everyone. It also helps to have people who will tell you your idea is bad, or that we should wait on x because y matters more to customers. Mine did, and I am grateful.

Give a "no" somewhere to go. When customers wanted custom work that would not suit everyone else, we built a page listing development agencies who work with our system regularly. The answer was no to the feature and yes to getting the customer helped.

That last part is what keeps the customer. A bare "no" ends the conversation. A "no, and here are people who can do it" leaves them better off than before.

If I had to give one piece of advice from all of this, it would be to never lose focus on your vision. Every custom request is a small vote to give it up.
