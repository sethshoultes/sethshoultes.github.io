I took notes at a WordCamp Las Vegas talk about getting media attention. A few ideas stayed with me, and they work for any small business, not just people who build websites.

Lead the conversation. Blogging is a way to lead, not just to post. The more you give away, the more people find you. If your only goal is more sales and page views, you are doing it wrong.

Be the person a reporter wants to call. The talk gave a short list of reasons you get picked: you know your subject, you are nearby and easy to reach, you give good sound bites, you bring an angle that helps the story, and you always add something for the reporter. Media outlets are hungry for content. Help them out.

Say it in one sentence. Most sound bites are a single sentence. Practice saying your point briefly. Record yourself and listen back, or join a group like Toastmasters.

Pitch like you work in PR, because you do. Use facts, numbers and quotes. Tag the outlets when you share your stories. Offer an exclusive now and then. Be persistent, but not a nuisance. And get known for positive reasons: make friends, and help promote their news to your own audience.

Make it easy to watch you. Reporters notice what other reporters cover, which can lead to more than one appearance. Train them to keep an eye on your social feed, and write to reporters, columnists, magazines and radio hosts directly.

If I had to boil it down to one line: be useful to the people who tell stories, and they will tell yours.

These are my notes from the talk, and the ideas belong to its speaker.
