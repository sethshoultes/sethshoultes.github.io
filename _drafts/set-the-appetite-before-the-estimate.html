--- title: "Set the Appetite Before the Estimate" subtitle: "Most projects start with a design and end with a number. There is a better order." description: "Most projects start with a design and end with a number. There is a better order." read_time: "5 min read" ---

Most of us plan a project the same way. We sketch what we want, hand it to someone, and ask how long it will take. The answer is a number, and the number becomes a promise.

The trouble is that the more detail you put into the sketch, the harder the number is to get right. When you draw every screen before anyone has started, you have decided a lot of things too early, and you have left the people building it no room to find a simpler way.

The team at Basecamp has a better order in Shape Up. They call it an appetite. An estimate starts with a design and ends with a number. An appetite starts with a number and ends with a design. You decide up front how much time this is worth, and then you shape the work to fit.

That small flip changes the conversation. Instead of "how long will this take?", the question is "what is the best version we can build in two weeks?" or "in six?" The time box forces the hard choices early: what is the core of this, and what is nice to have.

If you run a small business, here is how I would use it. Before you ask anyone for a quote, write down what the problem is worth to you and how much time you are willing to spend on it. Then ask for the best answer that fits inside that. You will get a smaller, sharper project, and you will find out fast if what you want cannot be done in the time you can afford.

This is Basecamp's idea, and their book is free to read online. These are my notes on what stuck with me.
