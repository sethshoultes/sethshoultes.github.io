--- title: "Fixed Time, Variable Scope" subtitle: "When a project runs late, you can cut the time or cut the work. Only one of those works." description: "When a project runs late, you can cut the time or cut the work. Only one of those works." read_time: "5 min read" ---

Every project hits the same moment. Halfway through, it is clear there is more to do than there is time to do it. You can move the deadline, or you can change what you are building.

Moving the deadline feels kind. It is also how a two-week job turns into a two-month job. Basecamp's Shape Up goes the other way. They fix the time and let the scope move. The time box is the part you protect.

It sounds backwards until you try it. When the end date is fixed, everyone starts asking the right question: is this core to the project, or is it peripheral? The team makes those calls as they go, with real information, instead of arguing about them in a planning meeting.

Two warnings from their writing are worth keeping. First, watch out for grab-bags. If someone says "redesign the Files section", that is not a project. It is a pile, and nobody can say where it starts or ends. Narrow it down until it has a clear edge. Second, look for rabbit holes. Walk through the idea slowly, step by step, and ask whether it needs something new we have never done, or whether we are assuming the pieces fit together.

And one question I now ask a lot, from the same book: do not ask "is this possible?" In software, everything is possible and nothing is free. Ask "is this possible in six weeks?" That is a very different question.

The idea is Basecamp's. These are my notes on how to use it.
