--- title: "One Priority a Week, and the Daily Check-in" subtitle: "A small rhythm that keeps a team pointed the same way." description: "A small rhythm that keeps a team pointed the same way." read_time: "4 min read" ---

Teams rarely fail because they are lazy. They fail because everyone is working on something slightly different and nobody can see it.

Here is a rhythm that fixes a lot of that. It has two parts.

One priority a week. Pick one high-priority piece of work and say what it is. The team focuses on one project at a time, and each person takes a separate part of it. If someone is not needed on that project right now, they work on their other projects. And critical bugs come first, before any new feature.

A daily check-in. Once a day, in whatever chat your team uses, ask everyone what they are working on today. That is the whole check-in. It takes a minute to answer and it keeps the priority visible.

Each week, three questions are enough:

  • What are you doing to move this week's priority forward?
  • What is blocking you?
  • What is everyone else working on this week?

There is one sentence I like best. Say it out loud on Monday: "The top priority for the team is X. What is everyone doing to move X closer to release?"

It is a small habit. But a team that can answer that sentence is a team that is rowing in the same direction, and that is most of the battle.
