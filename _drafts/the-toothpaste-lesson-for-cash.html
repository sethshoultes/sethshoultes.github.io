--- title: "The Toothpaste Lesson for Cash" subtitle: "Why a nearly empty tube teaches you more about money than a full one." description: "Why a nearly empty tube teaches you more about money than a full one." read_time: "3 min read" ---

Did you brush your teeth this morning? Did you think about how much toothpaste you used? Probably not. With a full tube, we squeeze without looking.

That is the lesson at the heart of a short piece in Shawn Van Dyke's Profit First for Contractors course. When the tube is full, we use more than we need. When it is nearly empty, we get careful and creative and make it last.

The course ties it to a rule named after C. Northcote Parkinson: the demand for something grows to match the supply. Put a lot of money in the bank account and spending quietly grows to use it. Put in less and you find a way to get by.

The practical idea is to arrange your money so that the account you spend from never looks fuller than it should. Set the profit, tax and owner's pay aside first, and run the business from what is left. When the spending account looks lean, you innovate. When it looks full, you overspend.

I like this because it asks for a change in setup, not in willpower. You do not need to become a more disciplined person. You need to make the tube look the right size.
